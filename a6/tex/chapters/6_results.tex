\section{Results}
This semiconductor laboratory experiment explores the characterization of an \ac{algao} thin film
grown on an r-sapphire substrate. To characterize this system, first the miscut of
the substrate is identified. After that, $\omega$-scans of substrate peaks are conducted
to estimate its dislocation densities. Subsequent $2\theta\text{-}\omega$ scans
provide information about the lattice parameters of the thin film and substrate. Finally,
an RSM scan visualizes the Ewald sphere directly.

\subsection{Miscut Determination}

\begin{figure}
	\begin{subfigure}
		{0.48\linewidth}
		\centering
		\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../plots/task_1_omega.pdf}
		\caption{$\omega$-scans of the $(024)$ reflection for
		$\varphi= \SIlist{0;90;180;270}{\degree}$.}
		\label{fig:task1_omega}
	\end{subfigure}
	\begin{subfigure}
		{0.48\linewidth}
		\centering
		\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../plots/task_1_phi.pdf}
		\caption{Peak position $\omega_{\mathrm{max}}$ as a function of $\varphi$, fitted
		to determine the miscut angle $\gamma$.}
		\label{fig:task1_phi}
	\end{subfigure}
\end{figure}

To measure the miscut angle $\gamma$ of the sapphire substrate, four $\omega$-scans
for different azimuthal angles $\varphi= \SIlist{0;90;180;270}{\degree}$ were conducted
for $\theta_{\mathrm{B}}= \ang{26.27}$, examining the sapphire $(024)$ plane. For
each scan, the maximum intensity $I_{\mathrm{max}}$ as well as the corresponding
angle $\omega_{\mathrm{max}}$ was extracted, as depicted in \cref{fig:task1_omega}.
As discussed in \cref{sec:miscut}, the $(024)$ reciprocal lattice vector rotates
around the $q_{\parallel}$ axis, resulting in an alternating projection at
different $\omega$ values. The data $\omega_{\mathrm{max}}(\varphi)$ can be fitted
as:
\begin{equation}
	\omega_{\mathrm{max}}-\theta_{\mathrm{B}}=\arctan[\tan(\gamma)\cos(\varphi+\varphi
	_{0})]
\end{equation}
The resulting fit, shown in \cref{fig:task1_phi}, yields an offset angle of $\varphi_{0}
=\ang{-24}$ and a miscut angle of $\gamma=\ang{0.18}$.

\subsection{Sapphire Rocking Curves}

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\begin{subfigure}
		[t]{0.48\linewidth}
		\centering
		\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../plots/task_2_006_omega.pdf}
		\caption{$(006)$ reflection}
		\label{fig:task2_006}
	\end{subfigure}
	\begin{subfigure}
		[t]{0.48\linewidth}
		\centering
		\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../plots/task_2_024_omega.pdf}
		\caption{$(024)$ reflection}
		\label{fig:task2_024}
	\end{subfigure}
	\caption{$\omega$-rocking curves of the symmetric $(006)$ and asymmetric
	$(024)$ reflections, each fitted with pseudo-Voigt peaks.}
	\label{fig:task2}
\end{figure}

Rocking curves were recorded for the symmetric $(024)$ and the asymmetric $(006)$
reflections to determine the coherence length and the dislocation densities, see
\cref{fig:task2}. The intensity graph of \cref{fig:task2_006} can be reconstructed
using three individual pseudo-Voigt functions. A possible explanation of this
complex shape is the formation of three distinct crystallographic domains. Alternatively,
the data could be interpreted as showing a substrate peak, a film peak, and a
single sub-domain. \Cref{fig:task2_024} displays two well-separated peaks. The
right peak corresponds to the substrate's $(024)$ reflection. The left peak could
be an artifact originating from the thin film, as its broad
intensity distribution is captured by the wide-acceptance detector.

The central peak of \cref{fig:task2_006} is located at \ang{20.887} and features an
FWHM of \ang{0.015}. Using \cref{eq:coherence_length} yields an upper bound for
the coherence length of $L_{\parallel}<\qty{902}{\nano \meter}$. As the
dislocation orientation $\mathbf{L}$ is defined parallel to the $(012)$
direction, screw-type dislocations form parallel to $(012)$.

A suitable Burgers vector must be a direct lattice vector parallel to $\mathbf{L}$.
Taking the ansatz $\mathbf{b}\propto \mathbf{b}_{2}+ 2 \mathbf{b}_{3}$ yields:
\begin{equation}
	u=\lambda\quad v = 2\lambda \quad w=3 \frac{a^{2}}{c^{2}}\lambda \simeq 0.4 \lambda
\end{equation}
A promising approximation is the $[241]$ direction, with a magnitude of \qty{28}{\angstrom}.
Using \cref{eq:rho_screw}, this yields a screw-type dislocation density of
$\rho_{\mathrm{screw}}=\qty{0.86e9}{\per\meter\squared}$.

The right peak in \cref{fig:task2_024} is located at \ang{26.29} and features an FWHM
of \ang{0.01}. A suitable Burgers vector must be perpendicular to $\mathbf{L}$ and is given by $\mathbf{a}
_{1}$, since $\mathbf{a}_{1}\circ (\mathbf{b}_{2}+2\mathbf{b}_{3})=0$. Although the
$(006)$ direction is not perpendicular to $(024)$, using \cref{eq:rho_edge}, one can
estimate an edge-type dislocation density of $\rho_{\mathrm{edge}}=\qty{73.9e9}{\per\meter\squared}$.

	\end{subfigure}
	\begin{subfigure}

		\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../plots/task_3_036_2to.pdf}
		\caption{$2\theta\text{-}\omega$-scan of the $(036)$ reflection}
		\label{fig:task3_036_2to}
	\end{subfigure}
	\begin{subfigure}
		[t]{0.48\linewidth}
		\centering
		\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../plots/task_3_omega.pdf}
		\caption{$\omega$-rocking curves of the $(006)$ and $(036)$ reflections}
		\label{fig:task3_omega}
	\end{subfigure}
	\caption{$2\theta\text{-}\omega$-scans of the $(006)$ and $(036)$ reflections
	used to determine the lattice constants $a$ and $c$, and a comparison of the corresponding
	$\omega$-rocking curves.}
	\label{fig:task3}
\end{figure}

$2\theta\text{-}\omega$-scans of the $(006)$, see \cref{fig:task3_006_2to}, and $(
036)$, see \cref{fig:task3_036_2to}, reflections were recorded to determine the lattice
constants $a$ and $c$ of the substrate and thin film, respectively.
\Cref{fig:task3_006_2to} displays two peaks, a small one centered at \ang{41.509}
and a prominent one at \ang{41.675}. The prominent peak corresponds to the
substrate $(006)$ reflection. The small peak does not originate from the $K_{\alpha
2}$ contribution as the measurement was taken with a monochromator. A possible
explanation of this peak is the thin film $(006)$ reflection.

\Cref{fig:task3_036_2to} displays a single peak, corresponding to the $(036)$ substrate
reflection. Rocking curves of both reflections were compared, see \cref{fig:task3}.

As stated in \cref{sec:coordinate_systems}, the scattering vector length can
be calculated as $\lvert \mathbf{q}\rvert =4\pi /\lambda \cdot \sin(\theta)$. Assuming
that both peaks relate to their respective $(006)$ reflection, the scattering vector
is related to the lattice constant as
$\lvert \mathbf{q}_{006}\rvert = 6 \lvert \mathbf{b}_{3}\rvert =6 \cdot 2\pi /c$.
This results in a substrate lattice constant of
$c_{\mathrm{substrate}}=\qty{1.29929(1)}{\nano \meter}$ and a thin film lattice
constant of $c_{\mathrm{film}}=\qty{1.3042(5)}{\nano\meter}$.

The magnitude of the $(036)$ reflection is related to both $a$ and $c$ lattice
constants:
\begin{equation}
	\lvert \mathbf{q}_{036}\rvert^{2}=k^{2}\lvert \mathbf{b}_{2}\rvert^{2}+l^{2}\lvert
	\mathbf{b}_{3}\rvert^{2}=3^{2}\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3a^{2}}+6^{2}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{c^{2}}
\end{equation}
Substituting $c_{\mathrm{substrate}}$ yields $a_{\mathrm{substrate}}=\qty{4.75925(1)}
{\nano\meter}$.

\subsection{Reciprocal Space Map}

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../plots/task_4_rsm.pdf}
	\caption{Reciprocal space map showing the full scanned region and a zoom onto
	the reflection.}
	\label{fig:task4_rsm}
\end{figure}

A reciprocal space map was recorded around the symmetrical $(024)$ sapphire reflection,
see \cref{fig:task4_rsm}. The left-hand side shows the full map, while the right-hand side displays the region of interest. A high-intensity peak can be observed
at $q_{\parallel}= \qty{3.61}{\angstrom}$, and a broader peak below at $q_{\parallel}= \qty{3.549}
{\angstrom}$, corresponding to the thin film. Using $c_{\mathrm{film}}=\qty{1.3042(5)}
{\nano\meter}$, the thin film in-plane lattice constant can be calculated:
\begin{equation}
	\lvert \mathbf{q}_{024}\rvert^{2}=k^{2}\lvert \mathbf{b}_{2}\rvert^{2}+l^{2}\lvert
	\mathbf{b}_{3}\rvert^{2}=2^{2}\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3a^{2}}+4^{2}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{c^{2}}
\end{equation}
This results in $a_{\mathrm{film}}=\qty{4.088}{\nano\meter}$.

The broadening in the
$q_{\parallel}$ direction originates from the finite thickness of the respective
layer. Broadening in the $q_{\perp}$ direction of a symmetrical reflection originates from the lateral
coherence length of the individual crystallites as well as the film mosaicity.
