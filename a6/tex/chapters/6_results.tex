\section{Results}
This semiconductor laboratory experiment explores the characterization of an \ac{algao}
thin film grown on an r-sapphire substrate. To characterize this system, first the
miscut of the substrate is identified. After that, $\omega$-scans of the substrate
peaks are conducted to estimate its dislocation densities. Subsequent
$2\theta\text{-}\omega$-scans provide information about the lattice parameters
of the thin film and substrate. Finally, an RSM scan visualizes the Ewald sphere
and demonstrates different broadening mechanisms.

\subsection{Miscut Determination}

\begin{figure}
	\begin{minipage}{0.48\linewidth}
		\centering
		\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../plots/task_1_omega.pdf}
		\caption{$\omega$-scans of the $(024)$ reflection for
		$\varphi= \SIlist{0;90;180;270}{\degree}$.}
		\label{fig:task1_omega}
	\end{minipage}
	\begin{minipage}{0.48\linewidth}
		\centering
		\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../plots/task_1_phi.pdf}
		\caption{Peak position $\omega_{\mathrm{max}}$ as a function of $\varphi$, fitted
		to determine the miscut angle $\gamma$.}
		\label{fig:task1_phi}
	\end{minipage}
\end{figure}

To measure the miscut angle $\gamma$ of the sapphire substrate, four $\omega$-scans
at different azimuthal angles $\varphi= \SIlist{0;90;180;270}{\degree}$ were conducted
at $\theta = \ang{26.27}$, examining the sapphire $(024)$ plane. For each scan,
the maximum intensity $I_{\mathrm{max}}$ as well as the corresponding angle
$\omega_{\mathrm{max}}$ was extracted, as depicted in \cref{fig:task1_omega}. As
discussed in \cref{sec:miscut}, the $(024)$ reciprocal lattice vector rotates around
the $\hat{q}_{\perp}$ axis, resulting in an alternating projection at different
$\omega$ values. The data $\omega_{\mathrm{max}}(\varphi)$ can be fitted as follows:
\begin{equation}
	\omega_{\mathrm{max}}-\theta=\arctan[\tan(\gamma)\cos(\varphi+\varphi_{0})].
\end{equation}
The resulting fit, shown in \cref{fig:task1_phi}, yields an offset angle of
$\varphi_{0}=\ang{-24.044}$ and a miscut angle of $\gamma=\ang{0.18(1)}$.

	\begin{subfigure}

		\caption{$(006)$ reflection}
		\label{fig:task2_006}
	\end{subfigure}
	\caption{$\omega$-scans of the symmetric $(024)$ and asymmetric $(006)$
	reflections, each fitted with pseudo-Voigt peaks.}
	\label{fig:task2}
\end{figure}

Rocking curves were recorded for the symmetric $(024)$ and the asymmetric
$(006)$ reflections to determine the coherence length and the dislocation
densities, see \cref{fig:task2}. \Cref{fig:task2_024} displays two well-separated
peaks. The right peak corresponds to the substrate's $(024)$ reflection. The
left peak could be an artifact originating from the thin film, as its broad intensity
distribution is captured by the wide-acceptance detector. The intensity graph in
\cref{fig:task2_006} can be reconstructed using three individual pseudo-Voigt
functions. A possible explanation for this complex shape is the formation of three
distinct crystallographic domains. Alternatively, the data could be interpreted as
showing a substrate peak, a film peak, and a single sub-domain.

The main peak in \cref{fig:task2_024} is located at \ang{26.29} and features an
FWHM of \ang{0.01}. Using \cref{eq:coherence_length} yields for
$\theta = \ang{26.29}$ an upper bound for the coherence length of
$L_{\parallel}<\qty{902}{\nano \meter}$. As the dislocation orientation $\mathbf{L}$
is defined parallel to the $(012)$ direction, screw-type dislocations form
parallel to $(012)$.

A suitable Burgers vector must be a direct lattice vector parallel to
$\mathbf{L}$. Taking the ansatz $\mathcal{B}=u \mathbf{a}_{1}+ v \mathbf{a}_{2}+ w
\mathbf{a}_{3}\propto \mathbf{b}_{2}+ 2 \mathbf{b}_{3}$ yields, with
$\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$:
\begin{equation}
	u=\lambda\quad v = 2\lambda \quad w=3 \frac{a^{2}}{c^{2}}\lambda \simeq 0.4 \lambda
	.
\end{equation}
A promising approximation is the $[241]$ direction, with a magnitude of \qty{28}{\angstrom}.
With \cref{eq:rho_screw}, this yields a screw-type dislocation density of
$\rho_{\mathrm{screw}}=\qty{0.86e9}{\per\meter\squared}$.

The central peak in \cref{fig:task2_006} is located at \ang{20.887} and features an
FWHM of \ang{0.015}. A suitable Burgers vector must be perpendicular to $\mathbf{L}$
and is given by $\mathbf{a}_{1}$, since
$\mathbf{a}_{1}\circ (\mathbf{b}_{2}+2\mathbf{b}_{3})=0$. Although the $(006)$ reflection
is not purely in-plane but forms an angle of \ang{57.61} with the symmetric
$(024)$ direction, \cref{eq:rho_edge} can still be used to estimate an edge-type dislocation
density of $\rho_{\mathrm{edge}}=\qty{73.9e9}{\per\meter\squared}$.

	\begin{subfigure}

		\centering
		\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../plots/task_3_omega.pdf}
		\caption{$\omega$-rocking curves of the $(006)$ and $(036)$ reflections}
		\label{fig:task3_omega}
	\end{subfigure}
	\caption{$2\theta\text{-}\omega$-scans of the $(006)$ and $(036)$ reflections
	used to determine the lattice constants $a$ and $c$, and a comparison of the corresponding
	$\omega$-rocking curves.}
	\label{fig:task3}
\end{figure}

$2\theta\text{-}\omega$-scans of the $(006)$, see \cref{fig:task3_006_2to}, and $(
036)$, see \cref{fig:task3_036_2to}, reflections were recorded to determine the lattice
constants $a$ and $c$ of the substrate and thin film, respectively. \Cref{fig:task3_006_2to}
displays two peaks, a small one centered at $2\theta=\ang{41.509}$ and a
prominent one at $2\theta=\ang{41.675}$. The prominent peak corresponds to the substrate
$(006)$ reflection. The small peak does not originate from the $K_{\alpha 2}$
contribution, as the measurement was taken with a monochromator. A possible
explanation for this peak is the thin film $(006)$ reflection.

\Cref{fig:task3_036_2to} displays a single peak, corresponding to the $(036)$ substrate
reflection. Rocking curves of both reflections were compared, see \cref{fig:task3_omega}.

As stated in \cref{sec:coordinate_systems}, the scattering vector length can be
calculated as $\lvert \mathbf{q}\rvert =4\pi /\lambda \cdot \sin(\theta)$. Assuming
that both peaks relate to their respective $(006)$ reflections, the scattering
vector is related to the lattice constant as
$\lvert \mathbf{q}_{006}\rvert = 6 \lvert \mathbf{b}_{3}\rvert =6 \cdot 2\pi /c$.
This results in a substrate lattice constant of $c_{\mathrm{substrate}}=\qty{1.29929(1)}
{\nano \meter}$ and a thin film lattice constant of $c_{\mathrm{film}}=\qty{1.3042(5)}
{\nano\meter}$.

The peak in \cref{fig:task3_036_2to} is centered at $2\theta=\ang{83.21}$. The
magnitude of the $(036)$ reflection is related to both the $a$ and $c$ lattice
constants:

\begin{equation}
	\lvert \mathbf{q}_{036}\rvert^{2}=k^{2}\lvert \mathbf{b}_{2}\rvert^{2}+l^{2}\lvert
	\mathbf{b}_{3}\rvert^{2}=3^{2}\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3a^{2}}+6^{2}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{c^{2}}
	.
\end{equation}
Substituting $c_{\mathrm{substrate}}$ yields
$a_{\mathrm{substrate}}=\qty{4.75925(1)}{\angstrom}$. Both substrate lattice constants
agree with the literature values of sapphire, $a=\qty{4.75925}{\angstrom}$ and
$c=\qty{12.9929}{\angstrom}$ \autocite{wsi2007hrxrd}.

\subsection{Reciprocal Space Map}

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../plots/task_4_rsm.pdf}
	\caption{Reciprocal space map showing the full scanned region and a close-up
	of the reflection.}
	\label{fig:task4_rsm}
\end{figure}

A reciprocal space map was recorded around the symmetrical $(024)$ sapphire reflection,
see \cref{fig:task4_rsm}. The left-hand side shows the full map, while the right-hand
side displays the region of interest. A high-intensity substrate peak can be observed
at $q_{\perp}= \qty{3.61}{\per\angstrom}$, and a broader peak below it at
$q_{\perp}= \qty{3.549}{\per\angstrom}$, corresponding to the thin film. Using
$c_{\mathrm{film}}=\qty{1.3042(5)}{\nano\meter}$, the thin film lattice constant $a$
can be calculated:
\begin{equation}
	\lvert \mathbf{q}_{024}\rvert^{2}=k^{2}\lvert \mathbf{b}_{2}\rvert^{2}+l^{2}\lvert
	\mathbf{b}_{3}\rvert^{2}=2^{2}\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3a^{2}}+4^{2}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{c^{2}}
	.
\end{equation}
This results in $a_{\mathrm{film}}=\qty{4.868}{\angstrom}$.

The broadening in the $\hat{q}_{\perp}$ direction originates from the finite
thickness of the respective layer. Broadening in the $\hat{q}_{\parallel}$ direction
of a symmetric reflection originates from the lateral coherence length of the individual
crystallites as well as the film mosaicity.

\subsection{Gallium Concentration}

The thin film is an $\mathrm{(Al_{1-x}Ga_{x})_{2}O_{3}}$ alloy, whose lattice constants
are approximated by a linear interpolation between the two members \ce{Al2O3}
and \ce{Ga2O3}, known as Vegard's law \autocite{vegard_konstitution_1921}:
\begin{equation}
	x=\frac{L_{\mathrm{film}}-L_{\mathrm{Al_{2}O_{3}}}}{L_{\mathrm{Ga_{2}O_{3}}}-L_{\mathrm{Al_{2}O_{3}}}}
	, \label{eq:vegard}
\end{equation}
where $L$ denotes either the $a$ or the $c$ lattice constant and $x$ denotes the
gallium concentration. Using the reference values $a=\qty{4.75925}{\angstrom}$ and
$c=\qty{12.9929}{\angstrom}$ for \ce{Al2O3} \autocite{wsi2007hrxrd}, and
$a=\qty{4.98}{\angstrom}$ and $c=\qty{13.43}{\angstrom}$ for \ce{Ga2O3} \autocite{pearton_review_2018},
the measured constants $c_{\mathrm{film}}=\qty{13.042}{\angstrom}$ and $a_{\mathrm{film}}
=\qty{4.868}{\angstrom}$ yield gallium concentrations of $x_{c}=\qty{11}{\percent}$
and $x_{a}=\qty{49}{\percent}$, respectively.

Both estimates differ considerably. The heteroepitaxial growth on the lattice-mismatched
substrate induces strain in the film, so that its lattice constants deviate from
those of a relaxed alloy. Evaluating \cref{eq:vegard} with $a$ and $c$ therefore leads
to different concentrations. The deviation could also indicate that the small
peak in \cref{fig:task3_006_2to} does not originate from the thin film, in which case
$c_{\mathrm{film}}$ and the concentration $x_{c}$ derived from it would not describe
the film at all.
