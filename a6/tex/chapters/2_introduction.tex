\section{Introduction}
Thin films are excellent systems for researching electroceramic materials. Modern
deposition methods enable the engineering of complex thin films, such as
multilayers, nanocomposites or layers with a graded composition. This results in
a variety of applications, including microelectronics, optics and information
storage. In most cases, the chemical and physical properties of the thin film
differ from those of its bulk counterpart. These differences include, but are
not limited to: geometry, grain size, preferential orientation, crystallinity, strain
and stress.

Most of these differences originate from the growth conditions that differ slightly
for each deposition process. It is therefore necessary to use tools to routinely
characterize newly deposited thin films. A useful and versatile technique for thin
films is \ac{xrd}. It provides information about the crystalline phase and lattice parameters.
With \ac{hrxrd}, further material characteristics such as dislocation densities,
residual stress and strain can be obtained.

\ac{xrd} is a non-destructive measurement technique that yields precise and
accurate results if carried out properly. To this end, a \ac{algao} thin film on an
r-sapphire substrate (\ce{Al2O3}, surface parallel to $(012)$ planes) is examined
in this laboratory. After alignment and miscut identification, $2\theta\text{-}\omega$-scans,
$\omega$-scans and reciprocal space maps are performed to determine crystalline
quality and lattice parameters.
