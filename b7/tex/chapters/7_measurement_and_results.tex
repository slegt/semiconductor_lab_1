\section{Measurement and Results}
\label{sec:measurement_and_results}

This report examines different properties of a laser diode. Current-voltage
characteristics were evaluated using the setup shown in \cref{fig:setup_iu}. Optical
power-current characteristics were evaluated in \cref{sec:iu_pi} with the measurement
setup depicted in \cref{fig:setup_pi}. The temperature dependence of the
threshold current is examined in \cref{sec:t0}.

The spectrum of the laser diode is analyzed for different driving currents. Based
on its magnitude, the diode behaves either as an LED, see \cref{sec:spontaneous_emission_spectra}
or as a laser diode, see \cref{sec:mode_spectrum}. Finally, the power density in
the active region is estimated in \cref{sec:power_density}.

\begin{itemize}
	\item power-current characteristics, measured at \qty{21}{\celsius}

	\item for low currents (up to $I_{\mathrm{th}}$), optical power is nearly zero
		\begin{itemize}
			\item pn diode acts as an LED

			\item no coherent light, large emission angle

			\item detector only measures small fraction of the total output power
		\end{itemize}

	\item for currents larger than $I_{\mathrm{th}}$, optical power rises sharpy
		\begin{itemize}
			\item critical current density is reached, every additional current is

			\item therefore, lasing starts, coherent light reaches the detector,
				resulting in linear relationship

			\item narrow emission angle

			\item the slope reads a differential efficiency of $(9.44 \pm 0.02)\,\%$
		\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Method}
\begin{itemize}
	\item Fit of $\ln p_{\lambda}$ to the line shape model on the high-energy flank
		only (\qtyrange{732}{775}{\nano\meter} and \qtyrange{726}{768}{\nano\meter}).

	\item $E_{\mathrm{g}}$ tied to the measured peak via $E_{\mathrm{peak}}= E_{\mathrm{g}}
		+ k_{\mathrm{B}}T_{\mathrm{c}}/2$, separately for each spectrum (peaks at \qty{782}{\nano\meter}
		and \qty{780}{\nano\meter}); only $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ and amplitude are free.

	\item Uncertainty of $E_{\mathrm{g}}$: one \qty{2}{\nano\meter} scan step at
		the peak. Uncertainties of $T_{\mathrm{c}}$: fit only.

	\item At \qty{9.9}{\milli\ampere} the peak is the amplified part of the line,
		so $E_{\mathrm{g}}$ from the peak is less reliable than for the LED.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Task 3 (\qty{0.1}{\milli\ampere})}
\begin{itemize}
	\item Broad line (FWHM \qty{20}{\nano\meter}): spontaneous emission only, LED operation.

	\item Model describes the flank over the whole fit window.

	\item $T_{\mathrm{c}}= \qty{268}{\kelvin}$, about \qty{27}{\kelvin} below room
		temperature (\qty{295}{\kelvin}). Expected:
		$T_{\mathrm{c}}\geq T_{\mathrm{lattice}}$.

	\item $E_{\mathrm{g}}= \qty{1.574}{\electronvolt}$ (\qty{788}{\nano\meter}),
		plausible for an AlGaAs active layer emitting at \qty{780}{\nano\meter}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Task 4 (\qty{9.9}{\milli\ampere})}
\begin{itemize}
	\item Line narrows to \qty{4.8}{\nano\meter} (only two scan points above half
		maximum): amplified spontaneous emission below threshold.

	\item $T_{\mathrm{c}}= \qty{222}{\kelvin}$, lower than at \qty{0.1}{\milli\ampere}.
		Not physical as a carrier temperature.

	\item Gain decreases with photon energy above the band edge and turns into absorption
		above $\Delta F$, which steepens the high-energy flank and lowers the
		apparent $T_{\mathrm{c}}$.

	\item The fit measures gain times spontaneous spectrum, not the carrier
		distribution alone.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Comparison and systematics}
\begin{itemize}
	\item Expected: $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ increases with current (carrier heating). Observed:
		decrease, so the \qty{9.9}{\milli\ampere} spectrum is dominated by gain.

	\item Both values below room temperature: spectral response of grating and
		detector not calibrated, tilts the flank in the same direction for both currents.

	\item $E_{\mathrm{peak}}= E_{\mathrm{g}}+ k_{\mathrm{B}}T_{\mathrm{c}}/2$ is exact
		only for $\sqrt{x}\,e^{-x/k_\mathrm{B}T}$; the $1/\lambda^{4}$ factor shifts
		the peak slightly, below the \qty{2}{\nano\meter} step.

	\item Result depends on how $E_{\mathrm{g}}$ is determined: with
		$E_{\mathrm{g}}$ free (LED window up to \qty{782}{\nano\meter}), $T_{\mathrm{c}}
		= \qty{277}{\kelvin}$ and \qty{228}{\kelvin}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Power Density in the Active Volume}
\label{sec:power_density}
% Task 6: power density in the active volume in laser operation from the geometry of the
% laser (task6.ipynb)

\begin{itemize}
	\item At \qty{19}{\milli\ampere}, the registered optical power is \qty{0.9}{\milli\watt}

	\item By estimating the active volume as $L = \qty{300000}{\nano\meter}$,
		$W = \qty{5000}{\nano\meter}$, $D = \qty{20}{\nano\meter}$ $\rightarrow$ $V =
		\qty{3e-17}{\meter\cubed}$

	\item the power density can be calculated as $P_{\mathrm{opt}}/ V = \qty{3.0e13}
		{\watt\per\meter\cubed}$ (\qty{3.0e7}{\watt\per\centi\meter\cubed})
\end{itemize}
