\subsection{Diode Current Spectrum}

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\includegraphics{../plots/photocurrent.pdf}
	\caption{Photocurrent $I_{\mathrm{ph}}$ of the four photodiodes as a function
	of wavelength $\lambda$. Markers indicate the maximum of each spectrum at $\lambda
	_{\mathrm{max}}$.}
	\label{fig:photocurrent}
\end{figure}

Photocurrent spectra of five photodiodes were recorded and are depicted in \cref{fig:photocurrent}.
As all photodiodes are silicon-based, the band gap of \qty{1.124}{\electronvolt} states
a minimal energy to excite electrons. This corresponds to a threshhold
wavelength of
$\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}=hc / E_{\mathrm{g}}=\qty{1103}{\nano\meter}$. Above this threshhold
wavelength, no signal intensity is measured.

Below the threshhold wavelength, a signal is measured. The photocurrent-spectra
can be grouped into two categories, based on their line shape: Diode three and
five peak at \qty{1000}{\nano \meter} while diode one, two and four around \qty{600}{\nano\meter}.
This difference is explained in \cref{subsec:comparison}. It should be noted,
that the photocurrent significantly drops for small wavelengths. This originates from
the decrease in optical power of the halogen lamp, see
\cref{subsec:reference_spectra}.

To present the spectral responsivity, a reference spectrum needs to be recorded. A
calibrated reference detector with single-point calibration at \qty{633}{\nano\meter}
for radiometric power measurement was used to determine the optical power spectrum.
To capture the thermal shifts of the halogen-lamp, the spectrum was recorded at the
beginning and the end of the experiment, see \cref{fig:reference}. As the lamp was
operating throughout the day, only a small shift is observed. Time-dependent effects
can be neglected.

The relative deviation of the optical power is shown in \cref{fig:reference_spread}.
As the intensity drops below \qty{550}{\nano\meter}, the signal enters the noise range.
Subsequent calculations only consider wavelength greater thatn \qty{550}{\nano\meter}.

\subsection{Spectral Responsivity and Quantum Efficiency}

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\includegraphics{../plots/responsivity.pdf}
	\caption{Spectral responsivity $R_{\lambda}$ of the four photodiodes obtained
	by normalizing the photocurrent spectra with the reference spectrum.}
	\label{fig:responsivity}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\includegraphics{../plots/quantum_efficiency.pdf}
	\caption{External quantum efficiency $\eta$ of the four photodiodes as a
	function of wavelength $\lambda$.}
	\label{fig:quantum_efficiency}
\end{figure}

Using the average reference spectrum, the responsivity
$R_{\lambda}=I_{\mathrm{ph}}/ P_{\mathrm{opt}}^{\mathrm{avg}}$ was calculated
and is depicted in \cref{fig:responsivity}. The responsivity does not depend on the
optical sources power and is therefore a better device characteristic. The band
gap still determines the highest wavelength threshold. THe maximum responsivity wavelengths
for each diodes are depicted in \cref{fig:responsivity}, they are similar to raw
maxima. The steep increase at low wavelengths is mostly gone, as it was caused
by the vanishing detector power.

The external quantum efficiency spectrum is plotted in
\cref{fig:quantum_efficiency}. Diode three features the highest efficiency maximum
at \qty{37.14}{\percent} while diode five features the lowest quantum efficiency maximum
at \qty{2.32}{\percent}.

Direct comparison between the quantum efficiencies is not meaningful, given the
high systematic error in the measurement setup. The photodiode mount was readjusted
after each photodiode installation. The detecttion area generally differs from
diode to diode as well as in comparison to the detector. Therefore, the external
quantum efficiency is only a rough estimate.

\subsection{Photodiode Detector Linearity}

For a fixed wavelength, the photocurrent was measured while the optical power
was variied using neutral density filters. As the theory suggests $I_{\mathrm{ph}}
\propto \P_{\mathrm{opt}}$, the following relations holds true:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{I}{I_{0}}=\frac{P}{P_{0}}.
\end{equation}
Using a double logarithmic plot, see \cref{fig:linearity}, the slope of a linear function
$y=mx+n$ indicates the exponent as $y=exp(n)x^{n}$.

Diode four shows a nearly linear scaling as $m=0.92$. Diode five however shows
two regimes, with an unphysical break between them at \num{2e-2}. While the second
regime features a nearly linear scaling with $m=0.94$, the first regime only
scales as $m=0.62$.

\subsection{Comparison between Si- and UV-Enhanced Si- Photodiodes}
\label{subsec:comparison}

Both, the Si-Photodiode (D3) as well as the uv-enhanced Si-Photodiode (D1) are silicon
based and therefore share the threshhold wavelength of \qty{1103}{\nano\meter}.

However, D1 has a maximum responsivity at \qty{558}{\nano\meter}, while D3 features
a maxmimum responsivity at \qty{898}{\nano\meter}. D3 features a single borad
peak while D1 looks like a step-function.

As the measurement does not enter the UV-regime (\qtyrange{250}{400}{\nano\meter}),
no statement can made about their responsivity spectrum. However, it is expected
that D1 features a significantly higher responsivity, as it's optimized for this regime.

However, this optimization leads to a responsivity loss in the near infrared
regime (\qtyrange{780}{2500}{\nano\meter}). This is a result of different diode
geometry and dopinc concentrations. The Schottky photodiode features a responsivity
falloff in the UV-regime attributed to the presence of an inversion layer in the depletion
region.

For an ordinary photodiode, infrared radiation penetrates deeply into the
material due to a reduced absorption coefficient and generation in the whole SCR
is observed. However UV radiation does not penetrate depp into the material and a
large proportion of electron-hole generation happens in the inversion layer.

Using higher doping concentrations $N_{\mathrm{D}}$ leads to a shrinking inversion
layer width as well as a shrinking space charge region width as both scale as $\propto
1 / \sqrt{N_{\mathrm{D}}}$. UV radiation still reaches the full SCR and electron-hole
excitation results in a current. However the SCR is too small for IR wavelengths,
radiation is only partially absorbed in the SCR.

Therefore, a decrese in IR responsivity is observed. Also different coating may
influence the responsivity. By comparing line shapes, D5 could also be an
ordinary photodiode, while D2 and D4 might be uv-enhanced photodiodes.
