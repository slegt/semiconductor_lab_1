\section{Schottky Photodiode}
\label{sec:schottky}

In a Schottky photodiode, light with energy $h\nu > E_{\mathrm{g}}$, i.e.\ above
the band gap energy $E_{\mathrm{g}}$, excites an electron-hole pair, such that
the excited electron resides in the conduction band while the excited hole
exists in the valence band. The excitation may occur inside the space-charge region
(SCR) or in the neutral $n$-region. Inside the SCR, the built-in electric field separates
both charge carriers and creates a drift current, such that only holes are
transported through the metal contact, while electrons move towards the neutral $n$-region.
Charge carriers generated in the $n$-region may recombine. As there is no electric
field present in this region, currents arise solely through diffusion.

The Schottky diode is one-dimensional along the $z$ direction, with cross section
$A$, as depicted in \cref{fig:schottky_diode}. It features three distinct
regions:
\begin{equation}
	\underbrace{ z < 0 }_{ \text{metal contact} }\quad \Big| \underbrace{ \quad 0 \leq
	z \leq z_{0} \quad }_{ \text{space-charge region (SCR)} }\Big| \quad \underbrace{
	z > z_{0} }_{ \text{quasi-neutral } n\text{-bulk} }.
\end{equation}
Light enters from $z<0$ and propagates in the $+z$ direction. The space charge region
begins directly at the semiconductor surface.

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{../assets/schottky_diode.png}
	\caption{Schematic of the one-dimensional Schottky photodiode.}
	\label{fig:schottky_diode}
\end{figure}

The goal is to find an expression for the photocurrent, depending on the photon generation
rate. To this end, a differential equation is created using the continuity equation
of \cref{sec:continuity}, together with the current densities of
\cref{sec:current_density}. The central idea is to evaluate the current
at the boundary between SCR and neutral region ($z=z_{0}$) and utilize the conservation
of the total current to obtain it at any position within the diode.

\subsection{Boundary Conditions}

As the electric field points towards the metal contact, electrons generated in the
depletion layer are transported in the $+z$ direction. Only holes leave the device
through this contact. Therefore, the boundary condition $j_{\mathrm{n}}(0)= 0$
is valid.

Let $p_{0}$ be the equilibrium hole concentration in the absence of optical excitation.
The electric field is assumed to extract all excess holes that arrive at $z_{0}$,
acting as a perfect sink. Far from the junction, the bulk is undisturbed,
as essentially all photons are absorbed beforehand. This justifies the boundary conditions
$p(z=z_{0})= p(z \to \infty)=p_{0}$.

\subsection{Continuity Equation in Depletion Layer}

The first goal is the evaluation of the electron current density at $z_{0}$. As the
electric field is strong inside the SCR and transports electrons away rapidly,
recombination in this region can be neglected. Additionally assuming steady state
reduces the electron continuity \cref{eq:continuity_n} to:
\begin{align}
	\partial_{z}j_{\mathrm{n}}              & = -eG(z),                                \\
	j_{\mathrm{n}}(z_{0})-j_{\mathrm{n}}(0) & = -e \int_{0}^{z_{0}}G(z) \,\mathrm{d}z.
\end{align}
Here, $G$ denotes the generation rate of electron-hole pairs, which is specified in
\cref{subsec:generation_rate}. Applying the boundary condition $j_{\mathrm{n}}(0)
=0$ yields:
\begin{equation}
	j_{\mathrm{n}}(z_{0})=-e \int_{0}^{z_{0}}G(z) \, \mathrm{d}z. \label{eq:j_n_scr}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Continuity Equation in Bulk Layer}

The second goal is to evaluate the hole current density at $z_{0}$. Define $\Delta
p(z)$ as the excess charge carrier density, such that
$p(z) = p_{0}+ \Delta p(z)$ with $p_{0}$ being the constant equilibrium charge
carrier density. Substituting the current density of \cref{sec:current_density} into
the hole continuity \cref{eq:continuity_p}, assuming a spatially constant electric
field, results in the transport equation:
\begin{equation}
	\partial^{2}_{z}\Delta p(z)-\frac{eE}{kT}\partial_{z}\Delta p(z) = \frac{U(z)-G(z)}{D_{\mathrm{p}}}
	.
\end{equation}
In equilibrium, the thermal generation rate $G_{\mathrm{th}}$ balances
recombination as $G_{\mathrm{th}}=p_{0}/ \tau_{\mathrm{p}}$, with the minority
carrier lifetime $\tau_{\mathrm{p}}$, i.e.\ the average time a hole survives in
the $n$-bulk before recombining. Subtracting this equilibrium pair leaves the net
recombination, which at low injection is linear in the excess density, with
$\tau_{\mathrm{p}}$ as inverse proportionality constant:
\begin{equation}
	U(z)=\frac{\Delta p}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}.
\end{equation}
In the following, $G$ denotes the optical generation rate alone.
This leads to a linear, inhomogeneous second-order differential equation:
\begin{equation}
	\partial_{z}^{2}\Delta p(z)-\frac{eE}{kT}\partial_{z}\Delta p(z) -\frac{\Delta
	p(z)}{D_{\mathrm{p}}\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}=-\frac{G(z)}{D_{\mathrm{p}}}.
\end{equation}
As the electric field is present only in the depletion layer, the second term vanishes:
\begin{equation}
	\partial_{z}^{2}\Delta p-\frac{\Delta p}{D_{\mathrm{p}}\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}=-\frac{G(z)}{D_{\mathrm{p}}}
	. \label{eq:transport_bulk}
\end{equation}
Define the diffusion length $L_{\mathrm{p}}=\sqrt{ D_{\mathrm{p}}\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}$
and use $'$ to indicate derivatives. Multiplication of the left-hand side with
$\exp(-(z-z_{0})/L_{\mathrm{p}})$ is equivalent to differentiation:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\left[ \exp(-(z-z_{0})/L_{\mathrm{p}}) (\Delta
		p'+\Delta p/L_{\mathrm{p}}) \right] \\&= -\frac{1}{L_{\mathrm{p}}}\exp(-(z-z_{0})
		/L_{\mathrm{p}})(\Delta p'+\Delta p/L_{\mathrm{p}}) +\exp(-(z-z_{0})/L_{\mathrm{p}})
		(\Delta p''+\Delta p'/L_{\mathrm{p}}) \\&= \exp(-(z-z_{0})/L_{\mathrm{p}}) (\Delta
		p''-\Delta p/L_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}) = -\frac{G(z)}{D_{\mathrm{p}}}\exp(-(z-z_{0})/L_{\mathrm{p}}).
	\end{split}
\end{equation}
Integration from $z_{0}$ to $\infty$ and application of the boundary conditions
$\Delta p(z_{0})=0$ and $\Delta p(z \to \infty)=0$ yields the expression below. The
latter condition excludes the exponentially growing homogeneous solution, so that
the boundary term at the upper limit vanishes:
\begin{equation}
	D_{\mathrm{p}}\Delta p'(z_{0}) = \left[ \exp(-(z-z_{0})/L_{\mathrm{p}}) (\Delta
	p'+\Delta p/L_{\mathrm{p}}) \right]^{\infty}_{z_{0}}= -\frac{1}{D_{\mathrm{p}}}\int_{z_{0}}^{\infty}
	G(z) \exp(-(z-z_{0})/L_{\mathrm{p}}) \, \mathrm{d}z.
\end{equation}
As there is no electric field, current only originates from diffusion:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		j_{\mathrm{p}}(z_{0}) &= -eD_{\mathrm{p}}\partial_{z}p \big|_{z_{0}} \\&= -e \int_{z_{0}}^{\infty}
		G(z) \exp(-(z-z_{0})/L_{\mathrm{p}}) \, \mathrm{d}z.
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:j_p_bulk}
\end{equation}
Note that the homogeneous solution of the differential equation $\partial_{z}^{2}
\Delta p-\Delta p/(D_{\mathrm{p}}\tau_{\mathrm{p}})=0$, together with the boundary
condition $\Delta p(z \to \infty)$, is given as:
\begin{equation}
	\Delta p=\Delta p_{0}\exp(-\lambda(z-z_{0})).
\end{equation}
Therefore, it is justified to write
\begin{equation}
	j_{\mathrm{p}}=-e \int_{z_{0}}^{\infty}G(z) \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta p_{0}}\, \mathrm{d}
	z.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Current Characteristics}

Adding the steady-state continuity \cref{eq:continuity_n,eq:continuity_p} gives
$\partial_{z}j_{\mathrm{tot}}=0$, so the total current density is constant
throughout the device. Therefore, it can be evaluated at position $z_{0}$ as
$j_{\mathrm{tot}}=j_{\mathrm{n}}(z_{0})+j_{\mathrm{p}}(z_{0})$. Substituting
\cref{eq:j_n_scr,eq:j_p_bulk} yields:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		j_{\mathrm{tot}} &= -e \int_{0}^{z_{0}}G(z)\, \mathrm{d}z - e\int_{z_{0}}^{\infty}
		G(z) \exp(-(z-z_{0})/L_{\mathrm{p}}) \, \mathrm{d}z \\&= -e \int_{0}^{\infty}G(z)
		j(z) \, \mathrm{d}z,
	\end{split}
\end{equation}
with the point source current:
\begin{equation}
	j(z)=
	\begin{cases}
		1                               & z \in(0, z_{0}]      \\
		\exp(-(z-z_{0})/L_{\mathrm{p}}) & z \in(z_{0}, \infty),
	\end{cases}
\end{equation}
induced by the point source at position $z$. Multiplication with the cross section
$A$ results in an expression for the current:
\begin{equation}
	I_{\mathrm{ph}}=Aj_{\mathrm{tot}}= -eA \int_{0}^{\infty}G(z)j(z) \, \mathrm{d}z
	. \label{eq:photocurrent}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Definition of the Generation Rate}
\label{subsec:generation_rate}

The generation rate $G$ of electron-hole pairs is proportional to the photon absorption
rate $U_{\mathrm{ph}}$, with the internal quantum efficiency $\eta$ as
proportionality constant $G=\eta U_{\mathrm{ph}}$. Due to \cref{eq:photon_continuity_steady},
$U_{\mathrm{ph}}=-\mathrm{d}\Phi/\mathrm{d}z$. An expression for the flux is given
by the Lambert-Beer law of \cref{sec:lambert_beer}, which leads to:
\begin{equation}
	G(z)=\eta \Phi_{0}\alpha(h\nu, z) \exp\left( -\int_{0}^{z}\alpha(h \nu, z') \, \mathrm{d}
	z' \right). \label{eq:generation_rate}
\end{equation}
