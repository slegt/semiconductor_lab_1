\section{Schottky Photodiode}
\label{sec:schottky}

In a Schottky photodiode, light with energy $h\nu > E_{\mathrm{g}}$, i.e. above
the band gap energy $E_{\mathrm{g}}$, excites an electron-hole pair, such that
the excited electron resides in the conduction band while the excited hole
exists in the valence band. The excitation may occur inside the space-charge region
(SCR) or in the neutral $n$-region. Inside the SCR, the built-in electric field separates
both charge carriers and creates a drift current, such that only holes are
transported through the metal contact, while electrons move towards the neutral $n$-region.
Charge carriers generated in the $n$-region may recombine. As there is no electric
field present in this region, currents only establish through diffusion.

The Schottky diode is one-dimensional along the $z$ direction, with cross section
$A$, as depicted in \cref{fig:schottky_diode}. It features three distinct regions
as
\begin{equation}
	\underbrace{ z < 0 }_{ \text{metal contact} }\quad \Big| \underbrace{ \quad 0 \leq
	z \leq z_{0} \quad }_{ \text{space-charge region (SCR)} }\Big| \quad \underbrace{
	z > z_{0} }_{ \text{quasi-neutral } n\text{-bulk} }.
\end{equation}
Light enters from $z<0$ and propagates in the $+z$ direction. The space charge
region directly begins at the semiconductor surface.

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{../assets/schottky_diode.png}
	\caption{Schematic of the one-dimensional Schottky photodiode.}
	\label{fig:schottky_diode}
\end{figure}

The goal is to find an expression for the photocurrent, depending on the photon
generation rate. To this end, a differential equation is created using the
continuity equation of \cref{sec:continuity}, together with the current densities
of \cref{sec:current_density}. The central idea is to utilize the conservation
of the total current to probe the current specifically at the boundary between
SCR and neutral region ($z=z_{0}$) to obtain the current anywhere through the
diode.

\subsection{Boundary Conditions}

As the electric field points towards the metal contact, electrons generated in
the depletion layer are transported in $+z$ direction. Only holes leave the device
through this contact. Therefore, the boundary condition $j_{\mathrm{n}}(0)= 0$ is
valid.

Let $p_{0}$ be the equilibrium hole concentration, without any optical
excitation. The electric field is assumed to extract all excess holes that arrive
at $z_{0}$, acting as a perfect sink. Far away from the junction, the bulk is
undisturbed, as effectively all photons are absorbed beforehand. This justifies
the boundary conditions $p(z=z_{0})= p(z \to \infty)=p_{0}$.

\subsection{Continuity Equation in Depletion Layer}

The first goal is the evaluation of the electron current density at $z_{0}$. As
the electric field is strong inside the SCR and transports electrons away
rapidly, recombination in this region can be neglected. Additionally assuming
steady state reduces the electron continuity \cref{eq:continuity_n} to:
\begin{align}
	\partial_{z}j_{\mathrm{n}}              & = -eG(z),                                \\
	j_{\mathrm{n}}(z_{0})-j_{\mathrm{n}}(0) & = -e \int_{0}^{z_{0}}G(z) \,\mathrm{d}z.
\end{align}
Here, $G$ denotes the generation rate of electron-hole pairs, which specified in \cref{subsec:generation_rate}.
Applying the boundary condition $j_{\mathrm{n}}(0)=0$ yields:
\begin{equation}
	j_{\mathrm{n}}(z_{0})=-e \int_{0}^{z_{0}}G(z) \, \mathrm{d}z. \label{eq:j_n_scr}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Continuity Equation in Bulk Layer}

The goal is to evaluate the hole current density at $z_{0}$. Define
$\Delta p(z)$ as the excess charge carrier density, such that $p(z) = p_{0}+ \Delta
p(z)$ with $p_{0}$ being the constant equilibrium charge carrier density.
Substituting the current density of \cref{sec:current_density} into the hole
continuity \cref{eq:continuity_p} results in the transport equation:
\begin{equation}
	\partial^{2}_{z}\Delta p(z)-\frac{eE}{kT}\partial_{z}\Delta p(z) = \frac{U(z)-G(z)}{D_{\mathrm{p}}}
	.
\end{equation}
At low injection the net recombination is linear in $\Delta p$, with
proportionality constant $\tau_{\mathrm{p}}$:
\begin{equation}
	U(z)=\frac{\Delta p}{\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}.
\end{equation}
This leads to a linear, inhomogeneous second order differential equation:
\begin{equation}
	\partial_{z}^{2}\Delta p(z)-\frac{eE}{kT}\partial_{z}\Delta p(z) -\frac{\Delta
	p(z)}{D_{\mathrm{p}}\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}=-\frac{G(z)}{D_{\mathrm{p}}}.
\end{equation}
As the electric field is present only in the depletion layer, the second term
vanishes:
\begin{equation}
	\partial_{z}^{2}\Delta p-\frac{\Delta p}{D_{\mathrm{p}}\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}=-\frac{G(z)}{D_{\mathrm{p}}}
	. \label{eq:transport_bulk}
\end{equation}
Define $\lambda=1 / \sqrt{ D_{\mathrm{p}}\tau_{\mathrm{p}}}$ and use $'$ to indicate
derivatives. Multiplication of the left hand side with $\exp(-\lambda(z-z_{0}))$
is equivalent to differentiation:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\left[ \exp(-\lambda(z-z_{0})) (\Delta p'+\lambda
		\Delta p) \right] \\&= -\lambda \exp(-\lambda(z-z_{0}))(\Delta p'+\lambda\Delta
		p) +\exp(-\lambda(z-z_{0})) (\Delta p''+\lambda\Delta p') \\&= \exp(-\lambda(
		z-z_{0})) (\Delta p'' -\lambda^{2}\Delta p) = -\frac{G(z)}{D_{\mathrm{p}}}\exp
		(-\lambda(z-z_{0})).
	\end{split}
\end{equation}
Integration from $z_{0}$ to $\infty$ yields:
\begin{equation}
	\left[ \exp(-\lambda(z-z_{0})) (\Delta p'+\lambda\Delta p) \right]^{\infty}_{z_{0}}
	= -\frac{1}{D_{\mathrm{p}}}\int_{z_{0}}^{\infty}G(z) \exp(-\lambda(z-z_{0})) \,
	\mathrm{d}z.
\end{equation}
For $z \to \infty$, the exponential term vanishes, while at $z=z_{0}$, it reduces
to 1 and $\Delta p$ vanishes. The equation simplifies to:
\begin{equation}
	D_{\mathrm{p}}\Delta p'(z_{0}) = \int_{z_{0}}^{\infty}G(z) \exp(-\lambda(z-z_{0}
	)) \, \mathrm{d}z.
\end{equation}
As there is no electric field, current only originates from diffusion:
\begin{equation}
	j_{\mathrm{p}}&= -eD_{\mathrm{p}}\partial_{z}p \\&= -e \int_{z_{0}}^{\infty}G(z
	) \exp(-\lambda(z-z_{0})) \, \mathrm{d}z. \label{eq:j_p_bulk}
\end{equation}
Note that the homogeneous solution of the differential equation $\partial_{z}^{2}
\Delta p-\Delta p/(D_{\mathrm{p}}\tau_{\mathrm{p}})=0$ together with the boundary
condition $\Delta p(z \to \infty)$ is given as
\begin{equation}
	\Delta p=\Delta p_{0}\exp(-\lambda(z-z_{0})).
\end{equation}
Therefore, it is justified to write
\begin{equation}
	j_{\mathrm{p}}=-e \int_{z_{0}}^{\infty}G(z) \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta p_{0}}\, \mathrm{d}
	z.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Current Characteristics}

The total current density is constant throughout the device. Therefore, it can
be evaluated at position $z_{0}$ as
$j_{\mathrm{tot}}=j_{\mathrm{n}}(z_{0})+j_{\mathrm{p}}(z_{0})$. Substituting \cref{eq:j_n_scr}
and \cref{eq:j_p_bulk} yields:
\begin{equation}
	j_{\mathrm{tot}}&= -e \int_{0}^{z_{0}}G(z)\, \mathrm{d}z - e\int_{z_{0}}^{\infty}
	G(z) \exp(-\lambda(z-z_{0})) \, \mathrm{d}z,&= -e \int_{0}^{\infty}G(z) j(z)\, \mathrm{d}
	z,
\end{equation}
with the point source current
\begin{equation}
	j(z)=
	\begin{cases}
		1                       & z \in(0, z_{0}],      \\
		\exp(-\lambda(z-z_{0})) & z \in(z_{0}, \infty),
	\end{cases}
\end{equation}
induced by the point source at position $z$. Multiplication with the cross section
$A$ results in an expression for the current
\begin{equation}
	I_{\mathrm{ph}}=Aj_{\mathrm{tot}}= -eA \int_{0}^{\infty}G(z)j(z) \, \mathrm{d}z
	. \label{eq:photocurrent}
\end{equation}
Note that the current originating from the SCR at $z_{0}$ is a pure electron current,
while the hole current from the neutral current originates from holes only.

\subsection{Definition of the Generation Rate}
\label{subsec:generation_rate}

The generation rate $G$ of electron-hole pairs is proportional to the photon absorption
rate $U_{\mathrm{ph}}$, with the internal quantum efficiency $\eta$ as
proportionality constant $G=\eta U_{\mathrm{ph}}$. Due to \cref{eq:photon_continuity_steady},
$U_{\mathrm{ph}}=-\mathrm{d}\Phi/\mathrm{d}z$. An expression for the flux is given
by the Lambert-Beer law of \cref{sec:lambert_beer}, which leads to:
\begin{equation}
	G(z)=\eta \Phi_{0}\alpha(h\nu, z) \exp\left( -\int_{0}^{z}\alpha(h \nu, z') \, \mathrm{d}
	z' \right). \label{eq:generation_rate}
\end{equation}
