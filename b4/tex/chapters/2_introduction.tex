\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Photodiodes play a prominent role in sensor technology, as they are used in a
wide range of optical measurement devices. Incoming radiation creates electron-hole
pairs within the diode, which are separated in the diode's \ac{scr}. This
results in a measurable photocurrent. Optical power measurement devices relying on
photodiodes are found across diverse fields, ranging from optical fiber
receivers in communication technology and optical data readout in CD players to
smoke detectors in safety engineering. Photodiodes are also utilized in spectroscopy,
as photodiode arrays resolve the photon flux spatially.

Due to their technological impact, the laboratory protocol discusses the operating
principle of a Schottky photodiode in \cref{sec:theory_basics,sec:schottky}. Key
metrics, such as the responsivity spectrum and photocurrent linearity for five distinct
photodiodes, were examined experimentally in \cref{sec:results} using the measurement
methods in \cref{sec:methods}.
